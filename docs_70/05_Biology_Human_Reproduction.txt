CLASS 12 SCIENCE — BIOLOGY Topic: Human Reproduction

Human reproduction is sexual and involves the formation and fusion of
male and female gametes. The male reproductive system includes the
testes, ducts, accessory glands and external genitalia. The testes
produce sperm and the hormone testosterone. Sperm formation, called
spermatogenesis, occurs in the seminiferous tubules.

The female reproductive system includes the ovaries, oviducts, uterus,
cervix and vagina. The ovaries produce ova and secrete oestrogen and
progesterone. Oogenesis is the process by which female gametes develop.

The menstrual cycle is a series of changes in the ovaries and uterus,
typically lasting about 28 days, although normal cycle length varies.
The follicular phase involves follicle development and rising oestrogen.
Ovulation is triggered by a surge in luteinising hormone (LH). During
the luteal phase, the corpus luteum secretes progesterone, which helps
maintain the uterine lining.

Fertilisation usually occurs in the ampullary-isthmic region of the
oviduct. A sperm and an ovum fuse to form a diploid zygote. The zygote
undergoes cleavage and develops into a blastocyst, which implants in the
uterine endometrium.

After implantation, the placenta develops as an exchange organ between
maternal and foetal tissues. It supplies nutrients and oxygen, removes
waste products and produces hormones that help maintain pregnancy.

Parturition is childbirth. It involves coordinated uterine contractions
and hormonal signals. Lactation is milk production after childbirth;
prolactin supports milk synthesis, while oxytocin helps in milk
ejection.

Key stages: 1. Gamete formation 2. Ovulation and fertilisation 3.
Cleavage and blastocyst formation 4. Implantation and embryonic
development 5. Parturition and lactation

This topic is important for understanding reproductive health,
development and human biology.
