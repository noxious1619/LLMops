CLASS 12 SCIENCE — BIOLOGY Topic: Molecular Basis of Inheritance

The molecular basis of inheritance explains how genetic information is
stored, copied and expressed. DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the
hereditary material in most organisms. It is made of nucleotides, each
containing a deoxyribose sugar, a phosphate group and a nitrogenous
base.

DNA usually forms a double helix with two antiparallel strands. Adenine
pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, while guanine pairs with
cytosine through three. This complementary pairing allows accurate
replication.

DNA replication is semi-conservative: each daughter DNA molecule
contains one parental strand and one newly synthesised strand. Helicase
separates the strands, while DNA polymerase adds nucleotides in the 5′
to 3′ direction. The leading strand is synthesised continuously; the
lagging strand is made in short Okazaki fragments that are joined by DNA
ligase.

Gene expression involves transcription and translation. During
transcription, RNA polymerase uses a DNA template to synthesise RNA. In
eukaryotes, the primary RNA transcript is processed by capping, tailing
and splicing to form mature mRNA.

During translation, ribosomes read mRNA codons. Transfer RNA molecules
bring amino acids corresponding to the codons, and the ribosome links
them into a polypeptide. The genetic code is nearly universal,
degenerate and unambiguous.

Mutations are changes in genetic material. They may arise spontaneously
or due to mutagens such as certain chemicals and radiation. Mutations
can be harmful, beneficial or neutral, and they contribute to genetic
variation.

Key terms: • Replication: copying of DNA. • Transcription: synthesis of
RNA from DNA. • Translation: synthesis of protein using mRNA. • Codon: a
three-nucleotide sequence in mRNA. • Mutation: a heritable change in
genetic sequence.

Understanding these processes helps explain inheritance, evolution,
genetic disorders and biotechnology.
