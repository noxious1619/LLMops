CLASS 12 SCIENCE — PHYSICS Topic: Current Electricity

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a
conductor. If charge Q flows in time t, current is I = Q/t. Its SI unit
is the ampere (A). Conventional current flows from higher to lower
potential, opposite to the direction of electron drift in a metal.

Ohm’s law states that, at constant temperature and other physical
conditions, current through a conductor is directly proportional to the
potential difference across it: V = IR. The constant R is resistance,
measured in ohms (Ω).

Resistance depends on the material, length and cross-sectional area of a
conductor. R = ρL/A, where ρ is resistivity. Longer wires have greater
resistance, while thicker wires have lower resistance.

Resistors in series carry the same current and have equivalent
resistance Rₛ = R₁ + R₂ + …. In parallel, each resistor has the same
potential difference and 1/Rₚ = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ….

Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is consumed: P =
VI. Using Ohm’s law, P = I²R = V²/R. Electrical energy consumed over
time t is W = Pt.

Kirchhoff’s junction rule follows conservation of charge: total current
entering a junction equals total current leaving it. The loop rule
follows conservation of energy: the algebraic sum of potential
differences around a closed loop is zero.

Key formulas: • Current: I = Q/t • Ohm’s law: V = IR • Resistance: R =
ρL/A • Series: Rₛ = R₁ + R₂ + … • Parallel: 1/Rₚ = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + … •
Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R

These principles are used in household wiring, electrical appliances,
circuit design and power distribution.
