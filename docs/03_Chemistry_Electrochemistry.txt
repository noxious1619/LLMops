CLASS 12 SCIENCE — CHEMISTRY Topic: Electrochemistry

Electrochemistry studies the relationship between chemical reactions and
electrical energy. It mainly deals with redox reactions, electrochemical
cells and the movement of ions.

A galvanic (voltaic) cell converts the chemical energy of a spontaneous
redox reaction into electrical energy. Oxidation occurs at the anode and
reduction occurs at the cathode. In a galvanic cell, the anode is
negative and the cathode is positive. Electrons travel through the
external circuit from anode to cathode.

A salt bridge completes the internal circuit, maintains electrical
neutrality and reduces liquid-junction potential. Cell potential is the
potential difference between the cathode and anode. Under standard
conditions, E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode, using standard reduction
potentials.

The Nernst equation relates electrode potential to concentration. At 298
K, for a cell reaction involving n electrons, Ecell = E°cell −
(0.0591/n) log Q, where Q is the reaction quotient.

An electrolytic cell uses an external source of electrical energy to
drive a non-spontaneous chemical reaction. Electrolysis is used in metal
extraction, electroplating and the manufacture of chemicals.

Faraday’s first law states that the amount of substance deposited or
liberated at an electrode is proportional to the quantity of electricity
passed: m ∝ Q, where Q = It. Faraday’s second law compares the amounts
of different substances produced by the same quantity of electricity.

Conductivity measures a solution’s ability to conduct electricity. Molar
conductivity is the conductivity of the volume of solution containing
one mole of electrolyte.

Important distinctions: • Galvanic cell: spontaneous reaction; chemical
energy becomes electrical energy. • Electrolytic cell: non-spontaneous
reaction; electrical energy drives chemistry. • Anode: oxidation. •
Cathode: reduction.

Electrochemistry is important in batteries, fuel cells, corrosion
prevention, electroplating and industrial chemical production.
